# Markings atlas

`assets/textures/markings/markings_albedo.png`: 1024 × 1024, RGBA (straight alpha), used by
`assets/materials/markings.tres`. Generated by `markings_atlas.py`; the rects below live in code in
`markings_layout.py` (change them there, then regenerate the atlas and this table).

- Pixel rects are `x, y, w, h` with **y from the top** of the image.
  Godot UV = `x / 1024, y / 1024` (Godot's V also runs top-down). Blender UV: `v = 1 - y / 1024`.
- **Pieces** map their whole rect onto a quad of the stated real size.
- **Strips** span the full width, so they repeat along U: set U from `0` to `length / metres per atlas width`.
  Solid strips can use any U range (sample the middle, e.g. 0.25–0.75). V covers the strip height;
  the quad's width is the line width.
- Columns 1020–1023 and rows 0–3 stay empty, so nothing bleeds in when the texture wraps.
- Paint is white or palette colour (signs and tactile paving carry their own colours; use vertex colour white). The material multiplies the vertex colour
  (`vertex_color_use_as_albedo`), so white pieces can be tinted, e.g. digits in yellow for stand IDs.
- Transparent pixels carry the colour of the nearest marking (no dark edges when filtered).

**Curves:** the arc centre is the **bottom-left corner** of the rect (pixel `x, y + h`). The arc
starts on the bottom edge at radius R and ends on the left edge at radius R. Place the quad with
that corner on the curve centre; rotate / mirror the quad for the other three turns. Scaling the
quad changes the radius and the line width together.

**Paint, not slabs:** markings have no thickness. Place them at most 2 mm above the ground (or
project / bake them into the ground) and turn shadow casting off (`cast_shadow = off` in Godot).

**Line widths:** ICAO lets taxi centrelines be 0.15 m minimum; we use 0.3 m so they read from
the build view. Runway stripes (0.9 m edge and centreline, 1.8 m threshold bars, 9 m digits) are
real ICAO sizes for a 45 m runway.
